# Marketplace research

Study listings and reviews separately. Listings show supply, positioning, prices, bundles, aesthetics, and crowding. Reviews and internal business data are stronger—but still imperfect—signals of purchases, gifting intent, product problems, and repeated demand. Review counts, rankings, and seller activity are proxies, not verified revenue.

Record marketplace, query, category, geography, currency, retrieval date, observed listing sample, price range, review dates or velocity where visible, and platform limitations. Compare close substitutes and alternatives. Search for low-review saturation, copied aesthetics, price compression, durable pricing, complaints, unmet jobs, and gaps.

Create distinct commercial and saturation signals. Use `UNKNOWN` when purchase intent cannot be established. Opportunity conclusions must combine cultural relevance, commercial evidence, saturation, product fit, and risk; avoid magical scores. Surface trademark, copyright, character, logo, and phrase danger for the independent IP reviewer.
